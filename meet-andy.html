Meet Andy

Engineer, explorer, and scientist — building the tools and teams that venture where no one has gone before.

Pisgah Crater, CA

My career is dedicated toward the scientific exploration of extreme environments, often through the development of novel instrumentation and advanced robotic systems that my teams and I design, build, and deploy in the field — whether that field is a volcanic caldera, an ice shelf, the ocean floor, or interplanetary space.

I currently serve as Associate Director and Chief Systems Engineer of the Caltech/JPL Brinson Exploration Hub, a collaborative center where we rapidly execute ambitious projects and missions using custom-built agile spacecraft, instruments, and robots. I'm also a Visiting Associate in Planetary Science and Lecturer in Electrical Engineering at Caltech, holding a Ph.D. in Aerospace Engineering along with various degrees in Space Systems and Electrical Engineering from the University of Michigan, and a Master's in Geology from Cal Poly Pomona — a combination that gives me insight into some of the scientific questions worth asking and the engineering and operations needed to actually go answer them.

Three Fields, One Discipline

Whatever the domain, my work follows the same pattern: build a team to tackle a hard question, create the instrument or system, take it into the field, and operate under conditions that don't forgive shortcuts.

On active volcanoes, I've deployed dense fiber-optic seismic arrays to observe processes deep beneath the surface — from my own master's thesis on Kilauea to a Caltech-led Science paper on real-time eruption early warning in Iceland. (→ Earth Science)

Under polar ice and in the deep ocean, I've led the technical engineering team for BRUIE, a rover that drives upside-down along the underside of sea ice as an analogue for Europa, and carried that same ethos to Orpheus, a submersible built with Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution. Sometimes its larger vehicles, like supporting Nereid Under-Ice or diving with the crewed submersible Alvin. Sometimes its improvising a field rescue on an Arctic cruise with SpongeBob SpareParts. (→ Ocean & Ice, → Field Notes)

In space operations, that discipline scales to craft I can't touch once they've launched. Beginning in a scrappy CubeSat lab at U. Michigan, to a postdoc at the Japanese Space Agency, through Chief Engineer on NASA's MarCO mission to Mars, to Lunar Trailblazer, which reached space but never reached the Moon. (→ Space Systems)

Off the clock, that same readiness carries into outdoor adventure and emergency response: I'm an IFR pilot, a rescue diver, an EMT with the Pasadena Fire Department, and a supervisor at the remote Catalina Island hyperbaric chamber. I strongly believe in volunteering and community service and work to give back where I can. (→ Training & Certifications)

Let's Connect

If you're a student looking to turn classroom equations into hands-on hardware, I teach and mentor through Caltech's Practical Electronics for Space Applications and ASU's "MacGyver Engineering" course. (→ Courses)

If you're wondering whether my career path was planned in advance — it wasn't. There were plenty of rejected proposals, failed field tests, and things I had to explain to people I respected. (→ Fail Resume — including the parts that didn't work.)

If you're a professional engaged in scientific exploration, I'm always happy to compare notes on interplanetary spacecraft, field expeditions, or novel instrumentation.

Regardless — let's design what comes next.
