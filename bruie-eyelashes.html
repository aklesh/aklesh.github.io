My Eyelashes Have Icicles
2025-10-29
📅 2025-10-29 📍 Coordinate: 71.0365, -156.8129 🌡️ -25°F

Early last week (and extremely early in the morning) we left LA for our arctic home-away-from-home in Utiqiavik (Barrow), Alaska - the most northern point in the US. Over our almost three week stay, we’ll be deploying our “Buoyant Rover for Under-Ice Exploration” to local permafrost lakes to study the seasonal change of known methane seeps. While our work here improves our understanding of the local quantity and origin of the methane, our scientist partners are using this to inform larger models and understand what happens as the permafrost melts. We’re also looking at the region as a proxy for investigating (and discovering?) life on other worlds, like Jupiter’s frozen moon, Europa.

Monday’s field test got a bit chilly with the snowmobile ride back likely colder than -25 F, depending on windchill. We did see spectacular aurora - when still about 15 miles outside of town, we stopped the snowmobiles and turned off the lights to capture some photos.

This afternoon was a bit surreal. One of our team members was in the deep arctic tundra adjusting some equipment, talking to us via Starlink. Another group was in the lab commanding the two stations to communicate via underwater sound waves, while I was in a third location, virtually looking over their shoulder on a web browser. Upon success, we dialed in folks from Pasadena and had a four-way call, via Starlink internet, cell phones, and under-ice acoustic modems to control precursor robots that may, someday, travel to the edges of humanity’s known map to search for alien lifeforms.

Back to work on this end - daylight only lasts for about 7 hours right now and it’s quickly reducing.
