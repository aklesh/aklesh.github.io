My Eyelashes Have Icicles
2025-10-29
📅 2025-11-26 📍 Coordinate: 34.1561, -118.1319

BRUIE is under the ice, secure in its frozen tundra, inverted to the world above. Each day, we connect via Starlink to talk through sound waves, a close facsimile to how we might, eventually, explore Europa. As our primary exploration site ended up too shallow, we deployed further south (in still shallow, but deep enough waters) and have been consistently exploring for over three weeks. Energy usage indicates we could still be driving in February, but it’s likely the ice will freeze the vehicle in place far before that.

We had to (re)learn how to drive (on the snowmobile ride out, some loose batteries bounced amongst the electronics for several hours … not good, but field-repaired), and are now driving consistent paths. Just recently, we believe we’ve identified a methane seep, a target for our science team, and we’re slowly transmitting images to verify it exists. Someday, on Jupiters moon Europa, these seeps might indicate signs of life! Following identification, we’ll either sit and watch it for awhile, or head north to another seep field before freezing in for the winter.

Since all have returned from Alaska, we log in to co-operate the vehicle from spots around the US (and world!). The system has proven to be quite robust - my own travels have taken me further afield, yet I’m still able to call-in and control the rover remotely. After a red-eye into LA, I left again for Riyadh, Saudi Arabia for a workshop and conference. Unfortunately, it was the day of the airport slow-downs, so the Los Angeles-San Francisco-Frankfurt-Riyadh flights had some adventures (mad racing across the tarmac in an airline BMW to beat the connection constraints), but we made it.

In the US, space exploration is often can seem blasé, passed over (many times) because we engineers and scientists fail to communicate how basic research can connect to daily lives (and hopes for a better future). In Riyadh, it was refreshing to meet with students passionate about how they might be involved with space exploration… and how they could bring their country along. And in the same conversations, learned how to re-connect a mother camel with its abandoned young by causing it to faint (it works!). The context switching keeps you on your toes!

After a few quick days in Pasadena (and some truly unique encounters), it’s off again. The gear has been checked, the team is excited, BRUIE is toiling away up north, and I head a bit further south…
