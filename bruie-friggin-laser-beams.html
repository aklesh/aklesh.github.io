📅 2025-11-06 📍 Coordinate: 71.0365, -156.8129

One of the pushes this last year has been a massive upgrade to our under-ice rover, BRUIE. It’s been a multi-year endeavor to prep for this expedition with high expectations. Onboard we have a multitude of instruments, 4 cameras, 2 sonars, 12 computers… and lasers.

It turns out that these scaling lasers - parallel laser beams which provide a known fixed-distance between the two dots to get a sense of scale when we take pictures - also serve as brilliant indicators of the rover’s position when it’s under the ice when viewed from above. Driving around a glowing green dot in the middle of the arctic night was a fantastic experience. Imagine what it looks like from underneath the water, especially when it reflects off a bubble!

After nearly three weeks in Utqiavik, AK, it’s time to head south. BRUIE will remain under the ice until springtime. We’re operating remotely via Starlink - onboard batteries (about 80 Energizer C-cell alkaline batteries) should allow it to last several weeks, but it’s highly likely the ice will freeze down low enough to trap it first. Scientists are very happy, engineers are extremely sleep deprived, and no polar bears attacked - successful trip!

To cap off Tuesday’s final field day, some of the methane seeps were lit with a propane torch… nothing like small fireballs to a) scientifically show that the bubbles we are seeing are actually methane and b)… why wouldn’t you create late-night fireballs under an aurora-lit sky?
